# Echolocation Games — Research Brief

## TL;DR

- The genre is real but **commercially tiny**. Steam's own search returns only ~47 results for "echolocation" and ~31 for "lidar", and a large share of those are unreleased 2026–2027 projects. itch.io has 26 echolocation-tagged and 29 lidar-tagged games — nearly all free, jam-scale, single-player.
- **Scanner Sombre is the genre's canonical failure**: ~7,500 copies in three months against a ~50,000 break-even, despite 80s review scores from PC Gamer. Chris Delay: *"It's bombed in a big way."* ([pcgamesinsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/feature/66004/what-went-wrong-with-introversions-scanner-sombre/), [mcvuk](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/introversions-scanner-sombre-bombed-in-a-big-way/))
- The dominant player complaint across the genre is not nausea or confusion — it's **"tech demo, not a game."** That phrase recurs verbatim in Scanner Sombre's negative reviews.
- **Competitive echolocation has shipped exactly once at scale: Muffled Warfare (2018).** It has 72–87 reviews at ~82% positive, was abandoned in Early Access ~8 years ago, and its reviews' dominant complaint is *dead servers* — not that the mechanic failed. Several reviewers logged 189, 420, 433, and 655 hours. That is the single most encouraging datum in this brief.
- **Nobody has shipped co-op roguelike echolocation.** The nearest live attempt, Echophobia (1v3 asymmetric PvP + co-op, "Lidar-like sonar waves"), was still in Steam playtest as of March 2026.
- Recent commercial reality check: the best-performing pure-LIDAR game of the last three years is **LiDAR Exploration Program** (Apr 2025, $5.99, 289 reviews / 90%) — a $6 walking-scan game that grew out of a free itch prototype. Ceilings here are low unless you leave the horror/walking-sim frame.
- **Navigation confusion is the proven killer of longer sessions.** Perception (2017) scored Metacritic 56 largely for it; shipped mitigations are overhead maps and placed landmark beacons.

### 2. Deep dives

**Scanner Sombre (Introversion).** Rainbow color = *distance*, inspired by Radiohead's "House of Cards" video. Critically: **points are permanent** — the whole game is one particle cloud, every dot saved where you put it, millions by the end ([virtualarchitectures.co.uk](https://virtualarchitectures.co.uk/point-cloud-gaming-scanner-sombre-in-vr)). Commercial: 7,500 in 3 months vs ~50,000 break-even. Morris: *"everything kind of went well… The game itself is actually pretty good. It's not mind-blowing but it's alright"*; *"if you don't hit the big sales at launch, you are doomed to tiny sales"*; and critically: *"I wonder if that's because Scanner is a single playthrough experience"* — YouTube coverage did not convert to sales the way it had for Prison Architect. Negative reviews cluster hard: *"This is a tech demo, not a horror game"* (1.2 hrs); *"Stunning visuals but almost no gameplay"* (100 min); *"Scanner Sombre is a game best enjoyed through screenshots and videos"* (1.5 hrs); *"Each section goes on far too long… You just stumble through the same environments on repeat"* (1.2 hrs); and: ***"Why-oh-why did I have to hold that trigger button down for 80 minutes??"*** (1.4 hrs). Note the long tail: 3,478 lifetime reviews implies far more than 7,500 lifetime owners (rough Boxleiter → low six figures; *low confidence*) — the failure was launch velocity, not eventual reach.

**Muffled Warfare (Gattai Games, the Stifled devs).** FFA PvP, 3 maps, 8 weapons, everyone blind, sound both reveals the world *and* reveals you — silence is the stealth mechanic. Reviews: 62 positive / 10 negative of 72. Complaints, ranked: (1) **dead playerbase**, overwhelmingly; (2) pistol overpowered, sniper/shotgun weak; (3) maps reward camping and spawn-camping; (4) **"you can't tell when you're visible"**; (5) deathmatch-only, no team modes; (6) slow bullet travel makes aiming unintuitive. Verbatim: *"It's fun, but it's dead. I would not buy it right now"* (62 hrs); *"a great game to play with friends, but I don't expect the online community to hold onto it"* (52 hrs); *"no lobbies, website and discord link go to a gambling website, game hasn't been updated and twitter hasn't posted since 2019"* (189 hrs). SteamSpy owners: 0–20,000. Still labeled Early Access, "3–6 months" promised in 2018.

**Stifled.** Award-winning, mic-driven (speaking reveals you to enemies *and* the world), VR support, $19.99 — and only 78 Steam reviews. The Gattai arc (acclaimed single-player → ambitious PvP spinoff → both commercially invisible → studio silent since 2019) is the genre's cautionary trilogy.

**Perception.** Metacritic 56 (PC/PS4), OpenCritic 59, bottom 14% of games, 22% critic recommend; Steam 544 reviews / 73%. The failure mode is precisely the one to avoid: *"frustratingly getting lost and going in circles until you happen upon an object you missed the first ten times you walked by it"*, leaving players *"disoriented rather than alone and helpless."* Repetition was visible inside a 5-hour runtime.

**The Voidness.** $11.99, solo dev, 257 reviews (82%), all-time peak ~17 CCU. Solutions it shipped: **scanning turrets as placed landmarks**, scanner upgrades (vertical/horizontal sweeps, wider scan), stamina, and hiding with mic-detected breathing. Lists co-op — the closest thing to shipped multiplayer LIDAR horror, though not what the game is known for.

**LiDAR Exploration Program.** Cheapest instructive success: $5.99, 289 reviews / 90%, 8 locations, **a distinct color palette per level**, customizable scanner patterns, and — importantly — **an overhead map** as the anti-getting-lost affordance. Marketed honestly as *"quite simple and addictive gameplay, just walk and scan."*

### 3. Recurring design problems & known solutions

- **One-trick shallowness (the #1 killer).** Every commercial post-mortem lands here. No shipped solution in the walking-sim frame; the only games that escaped it added an opposing agent (Muffled Warfare's other players, Stifled's mic-hunting enemies).
- **Scan-input fatigue.** Holding a trigger for 80 minutes is documented as actively hated. Solutions shipped: auto-sweep upgrades (The Voidness), pattern presets (LiDAR Exploration Program), passive footstep-driven reveal (Dark Echo, Blind Bullet).
- **Navigation confusion.** Proven fatal at length (Perception). Shipped fixes: overhead map, placed beacons/turrets, per-zone color palettes as location identity.
- **Staleness vs. tension.** Scanner Sombre resolved it by *never* going stale — points are permanent — which killed the tension and forced scripted scares to carry the game. Nobody has shipped a decay model at scale. This is genuinely open ground.
- **Moving objects in a static cloud.** No documented solution anywhere in the shipped corpus. Muffled Warfare's version of the problem manifested as *"I can't tell when I'm visible."* This is the hardest unsolved problem and the one most likely to sink a PvP build.
- **Nausea.** Weaker evidence than expected. Complaints cluster on VR teleport locomotion in Scanner Sombre, not desktop sickness (*medium confidence* that flat-screen nausea is a non-issue).

### 4. Competitive / action attempts

Muffled Warfare (PvP, abandoned in EA), Blindsight: War of the Wardens (echolocation brawler, free student game, 54 reviews), Blind Bullet (2D shooter, free, 28 reviews), Echophobia (1v3 asymmetric, still in playtest March 2026, [SteamDB patchnotes 22214537](https://steamdb.info/patchnotes/22214537/)). Every one of these is free, student-made, or abandoned. **None died from the mechanic being unfun.** Muffled Warfare died of population; Blindsight was never commercial.

### 5. No-go signals — honest reading

There is **no evidence the mechanic can't carry action or multiplayer.** There is strong evidence that (a) the *audience* for perception-gimmick games is small and launch-week-dependent, (b) a PvP echolocation shooter cannot survive without immediate critical mass, and (c) horror/walking-sim framing caps you at a few hundred reviews. The strongest counter-signal in the whole corpus is the Muffled Warfare playtime distribution — 189, 420, 433, 655 hours. People will grind an echolocation arena shooter for hundreds of hours if anyone else is online.

## What this means for Blind Spot

1. **Do not ship PvP-first.** Muffled Warfare proves the exact death: a good mechanic with no lobbies. Ship the **co-op roguelike** first — it survives a low CCU, and it is the one configuration nobody has shipped. 1v1/2v2 come after a population exists.
2. **Auto-pulse must be the default.** The "hold the trigger for 80 minutes" review is the loudest single design signal in this corpus. Make manual pulses a *tactical* choice (silent, cheap, directional) layered over a baseline auto-sweep.
3. **The energy bar solves Muffled Warfare's #1 gameplay complaint** — "you can't tell when you're visible." Make pulse cost, sprint noise, and weapon fire *legibly* self-revealing. That coupling is the whole competitive game: seeing costs you being seen.
4. **Staleness is the differentiator and the risk.** Scanner Sombre's permanence killed tension; Blind Spot goes the other way. Ship a *readable* decay: fade toward desaturation with a persistent low-density "memory" skeleton so players never lose the map, only lose the detail. Full erasure will reproduce Perception's going-in-circles failure.
5. **Fund navigation affordances early**: per-zone color palettes (LiDAR Exploration Program), placed beacons/turrets (The Voidness), and a minimap or ping-marker. Every game that skipped these got "disorienting" reviews.
6. **Solve moving objects explicitly and visually.** Give live entities a fundamentally different render — real-time silhouettes, distinct color channel, never point-persistent. No shipped game has solved this; solving it *is* the trailer.
7. **Price and scope realistically.** The genre's recent winners are $6 with ~300 reviews. The escape hatch from that ceiling is that roguelike co-op sells on replayability, and Morris explicitly blamed Scanner Sombre's failure on being *"a single playthrough experience"* that streamers couldn't monetize.

## Confidence & gaps

- **High:** Scanner Sombre's 7,500/50,000 figures and quotes (two independent outlets); Muffled Warfare's abandonment, review split, and complaint ranking (read directly from Steam review API); Perception's critical scores; the itch.io/Steam landscape counts.
- **Medium:** The Voidness review counts (sources disagree: 238 / 257 / 308 across snapshots — it's growing); Scanner Sombre's lifetime owners (Boxleiter extrapolation only); Stifled's commercial scale (review count is a proxy, no dev statement found).
- **Low / unresolved:** Whether Gattai Games formally shut down (inferred from silence since 2019); Echophobia's playtest reception; exact desktop nausea incidence; whether any 2026 unreleased title (Orca Insurgency, Sona, Descent Horizon) is a direct competitor — worth a follow-up sweep before committing publicly to the concept.
- **Not found despite looking:** any GDC talk or formal postmortem on echolocation *rendering* design; any developer discussion of moving objects in a decaying point cloud.
