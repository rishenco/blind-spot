# Owner's brief v2 (verbatim, 2026-08-22) — reformalization after Proposal v1

I have formalized the game a bit more based on your proposals

Where: Web (for now)

The player:
- A robot that lives off energy generated by a reactor (inside the robot). The robot has a sonar and audio-perception systems.
- Single audio system for physical world approximation (sonar). All sounds made by you / enemies / environment generate a map (different sound intensity generate different density of blips). Different sources of sound generate different colors of blip.
- The active sonar (ping): consumes energy (amount depends on its augmentations), spawn a lot of blips, it is directed by default, activated manually by default. Two modes (q and e buttons): spacial sonar (360 degrees, smaller radius), directed sonar (for single directions, long distances)
- Movement: walk-mode, run-mode, jumps and climbing - very similar to The Finals.
- What consumes energy: movement, sonar

The enemy:
- A robo-dog - the only enemy for now.
- Make sounds, have three modes: patrolling, investigating (heard you but have never seen and intermediate mode between attacking and patrolling), attacking
- They are kamikadze - they blow up when coming close to you

The mission (core-loop):
- Environment: labyrinth-like, leveled, intuitive
- Set up you and your team arrive in the environment on your ship (airborne, something like Lethal Company)
- The objective: retrieve some materials from the environment and return to ship to return home (like Deep Rock Galactic)
- pickup upgrades during exploration and equip them

The map:
- Currently, I see the map as a silo - multi-layer circular tower (reference: Silo, the series, but without the central stairs)
- The silo has stairs connecting adjacent floors on the tower's sides
- needs to be navigatable

Problems:
- Core loop - uncertain. Current approach is similar to lethal company, repo and etc. They certainly work, but it has contradictions with our decisions: 1) they avoid good movement on purpose 2) they always go for crappy/sloppy vibe
- Combat - should it be there?
- Vertical gameplay - I was thinking that having 3 playable dimensions (like the finals) would be cool, but its unclear how to implement it here
- Blips at long range - if the map is large enough looking towards a large scanned can look very messy
- Overall visuals - easy to make them confusing, which breaks gameplay
- Parkour - currently has no clear gameplay, in Finals it is working because of complex map & destruction. We will not have destruction for sure, and map complexity might overcomplicate the navigation.
- Rogulite-ness - ??

Upgrades:
- Sonar sensor sensetivity - lets you hear over longer distance without increasing your ping loudness. Uses more energy
- Sonar ping power - more power to discover further
- Better movement - ?
- More energy generations

Ideas:
- A dog crashing through something inadvertently reveals geometry for you.

Clarifications:
- Core fantasy: fast, responsive movement through a world you only partially understand. Mastery is navigating efficiently and predicting routes based on enemy movement.
- Sonar: scans permanently build the map. Without scanning, you only perceive very nearby/contact geometry and sounds. Sonar costs regenerating reactor energy and also attracts dogs.
- Enemies: robo-dogs are mainly a routing/prediction threat, not targets for conventional combat. They patrol, investigate sounds, chase, and can traverse roughly the same spaces as the player.
- Movement + sound: good movement should stay genuinely good. Tension comes from incomplete information. Moving blindly is possible but risky because you can knock objects over and create noise.
- Level structure: drop the full circular silo for now. Prefer familiar rectangular industrial spaces with occasional multi-level rooms/shafts that create meaningful vertical movement and cross-floor sound.
- Core loop/progression is still the biggest unknown: generic "collect junk → sell → buy upgrades" feels too much like R.E.P.O./Lethal Company. Objectives should push you deeper, while optional upgrades/rewards create reasons to take risks and explore.
